I'm Matteo Spanio, an AI research engineer working on audio, music and multimodal generative
models. I'm a PhD candidate in Brain, Mind and Computer Science at the University of Padua, based
at the [Centro di Sonologia Computazionale](https://csc.dei.unipd.it/) in the Department of
Information Engineering. In the winter of 2025 I spent six months as a visiting PhD student at the
Music Technology Group of Universitat Pompeu Fabra in Barcelona.

Three threads run through the work.

## Audio cultural heritage

Magnetic tape degrades, and digitising it is itself error-prone: the wrong equalisation curve, the
wrong playback speed, a section spliced back in reversed. I work on detecting and correcting those
errors automatically, as part of the MPAI/IEEE-CAE Audio Recording Preservation standard adopted
by the IEEE as 3302-2022. In practice that means computer vision on the video of the tape running
across the playback head, locating splices and surface irregularities frame by frame, and signal
processing on the audio itself, including an envelope-derivative method for finding time-reversed
segments. Much of it was built with Audio Innova, the CSC spin-off, and I'm a member of
[MPAI](https://mpai.community), the non-profit body that develops the standards.

## Cross-modal generation

Taste and hearing are linked perceptually, and there is a body of neuroscientific work describing
how. The second thread asks whether a generative model can be taught those correspondences. In a
paper published in _Frontiers in Computer Science_ I fine-tuned MusicGEN on music paired with
detailed taste descriptions; in the listening study that followed, 111 participants judged the
fine-tuned model's output to reflect the intended taste more coherently than the base model.
The follow-up work is concerned with the less glamorous prerequisite: normalising the scattered
multimodal datasets the field depends on, and validating them perceptually.

## Symbolic music and LLMs

Notation is a language, and LilyPond is a textual one, which makes it an unusually clean probe for
what language models actually know about music. I build datasets and benchmarks in that direction:
a musicologically curated LilyPond corpus, and a benchmark for symbolic music generation and
understanding that asks whether an LLM can read and write notation rather than merely autocomplete
it.

Alongside the research I maintain [TorchFX](https://matteospanio.github.io/torchfx/), an audio DSP
library that swaps the NumPy and SciPy stack for PyTorch, so filters run on the GPU and slot
directly into a neural network. It was presented at DAFx 2025.

Before the computer science there was the clarinet, and it never stopped. I hold a master's from
the Conservatorio Cesare Pollini in Padua, 110/110 cum laude, and I play regularly with the
[Orchestra di Padova e del Veneto](https://www.orchestrapadova.it/) on clarinet, bass clarinet and
piccolo clarinet, as well as with several other orchestras and ensembles. The overlap is not
incidental: the problems I work on are musical problems, and playing is how I keep testing whether
the answers hold.
